Extract links and emails from text

The tool identifies common email address patterns and HTTP/HTTPS URLs. It removes repeated matches, while preserving the original draft. It is a practical extraction heuristic, not a full RFC email parser or a web crawler.

Check for false positives

Trailing punctuation is trimmed from URLs; unusual addresses, encoded URLs and uncommon delimiters may still need manual review. The tool never sends messages or visits extracted links.

Analyze repeated words

Switch modes to see words that appear more than once. This can reveal accidental repetition, not predict SEO rankings.

What the extractor finds, and how

The tool scans your text for two patterns. An email address is a run of letters, digits and the characters . _ % + -, followed by @, a domain and a top-level domain of at least two letters. A URL is anything starting with http:// or https:// up to the next space, quote or angle bracket. Trailing punctuation such as a full stop or closing bracket at the end of a sentence is removed from URLs. Results are deduplicated; email addresses are compared in lowercase, so Anna@Example.com and anna@example.com appear once.

How to extract emails and links

  1. Paste the text, or import a local TXT, CSV, Markdown, JSON or log file up to 2 MB.
  2. Choose whether to show extracted emails, extracted URLs, repeated words or the writing summary.
  3. Review the list for false positives (see below).
  4. Copy the result or download it as plain text, one item per line.

Use it responsibly

Extracting addresses from your own documents, exports or inbox notes is a common clean-up task. Sending marketing email to extracted addresses without consent is a different matter: many jurisdictions, including under the EU's GDPR and the US CAN-SPAM Act, regulate how personal data and commercial email may be used. Everything here runs in your browser and nothing is uploaded, but what you do with the result is your responsibility. Related tools: sort and deduplicate lines, text cleaner and find and replace.
