What is a LinkedIn profile headline?

LinkedIn's headline editing help explains that this field appears below your name and can describe your expertise rather than simply restating your current job title.

Is 220 characters the official current maximum?

220 characters is a commonly referenced length, not independently confirmed by the cited LinkedIn Help article. The limit may vary by product version or account; use LinkedIn's actual profile editor to validate before saving.

Draft a useful headline

Focus on role, audience and distinctive expertise. Overly stuffed lists of keywords are harder to understand than a short, credible statement.

What the headline is for

Your LinkedIn headline appears directly under your name on your profile and next to your name almost everywhere else: in search results, comments, invitations, messages and the “People you may know” panel. In many of those places it is truncated after a few dozen characters. So while this counter uses a 220-character reference (a commonly cited figure that we label as unconfirmed by LinkedIn's Help Center), the practical goal is to make the first 50 or so characters say who you are and what you do.

How to use the headline counter

  1. Write a first version that states your role and the value you provide.
  2. Paste it here and check the visible count against the 220 reference.
  3. Read only the first sentence or phrase, as it might appear in a comment or search result.
  4. Copy the headline into your LinkedIn profile editor, which is the final validator.

Tips

  • Use the job title people search for. Recruiters search for “Product Manager”, not “Product Ninja”.
  • Separators are fine, such as · or |, but avoid stacking many unrelated labels.
  • Keep claims verifiable. “Award-winning” invites the question “which award?”.
  • Revisit it when your focus changes; the headline is not a permanent title.
