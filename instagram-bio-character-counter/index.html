What is the Instagram bio limit?

Meta's own Instagram bio guide repeatedly recommends 150 characters. A bio may include spaces, mentions, line breaks and emoji; the app itself remains the authority on how text is validated.

Make a concise bio

Lead with who your profile serves, add one useful detail and include a clear next action where relevant. Do not spend limited space repeating the same taglines.

Emoji and visible characters

Complex emoji can look like one symbol but contain multiple code points. This counter shows both user-perceived graphemes and UTF-16 units; it does not claim to reproduce Instagram's private validation logic.

What fits in 150 characters

The Instagram profile bio is commonly limited to 150 characters, and this counter checks your draft against that reference using visible characters. It is a short space, roughly one or two sentences, so every word needs to earn its place. Line breaks, emoji, @mentions and hashtags all count. Your name, username, profile category, links and contact buttons are separate fields and do not use bio characters.

How to write and check a bio

  1. Draft three short lines: what you do or who you are, why someone should follow, and what to do next.
  2. Paste the draft here and check the count against 150.
  3. Look at the UTF-16 figure if you use several emoji; some emoji are stored as multiple units even though they show as one.
  4. Copy the bio into Instagram's profile editor, which is the final validator, and check how line breaks display.

Tips for a clear bio

  • Use the words people search for. “Vegan recipes” or “Lisbon photographer” tells visitors and Instagram search what the account is about.
  • Be specific. “Helping small bakeries sell online” is clearer than “Entrepreneur | Dreamer | Creator”.
  • Use emoji as markers, not words. Screen readers announce each emoji's name, so a row of decorative emoji can be tedious to hear.
  • Avoid “fancy font” generators. Stylized Unicode letters can display as boxes on some devices and are hard for search and screen readers to interpret.
