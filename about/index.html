ABOUT POSTCHARS

Simple tools for better writing.

PostChars is an independently operated collection of free online text tools and guides for writers, creators, students, marketers and social media teams. It answers one deceptively simple question, “how long is this text?”, the way each destination actually measures it.

Who runs PostChars

PostChars is designed, built and maintained by an independent web developer who writes for the web and builds browser-based tools. It is not owned by, funded by or affiliated with X, Instagram, Meta, LinkedIn, Google, YouTube, TikTok or any other platform mentioned on the site. There is no team of ghost-writers and no content farm behind it: the tools, the counting rules and the guides are written and checked by the person who operates the site.

The source code for the site is published on GitHub under the MIT license, so anyone can inspect exactly how a count is calculated.

Why it exists

PostChars started from a recurring frustration. A draft that a word processor called 270 characters was rejected by X; a text message that looked short was billed as three SMS; a Japanese ad headline that seemed to fit was flagged as too long. Each time, the cause was the same: platforms do not share one definition of a character. X weights links and many emoji, SMS switches encoding for a single curly apostrophe, Google Ads counts double-width characters as two, and Bluesky checks both graphemes and bytes.

Most free counters on the web report one number and leave you to discover the difference after publishing. PostChars tries to explain the difference before you post, in plain language, and to do it without asking you to sign up or upload your text.

What you can do here

The site offers more than thirty free browser-based tools, grouped by the job they do:

  • Counting: the character counter, word counter, detailed word statistics and the Unicode inspector for hidden characters and byte counts.
  • Platform checks: the cross-platform post checker, plus dedicated counters for X, Instagram, LinkedIn, YouTube, TikTok, Bluesky and others.
  • Ads, SEO and messaging: the Google Ads counter, SERP snippet preview, keyword density checker and SMS segment counter.
  • Editing: case conversion, line-break removal, text cleanup, find and replace, text comparison and readability scoring.
  • Guides: in-depth articles on platform limits and writing craft, from SMS encoding to structuring an X thread.

Editorial approach: how limits and guides are researched

Every number on PostChars is meant to be traceable. The standards we hold ourselves to:

  1. Primary sources first. Limits are taken from the platform’s own help centre, API reference or published specification (for example, Google Ads Help for responsive search ads, the YouTube Data API reference, the Bluesky AT Protocol lexicon, the Telegram Bot API and 3GPP TS 23.038 for SMS). Tool pages and guides link to the source they rely on.
  2. Official versus reference, labelled. Where a platform documents a limit, the tool says so. Where it does not, as with the LinkedIn headline field or a universal Facebook post cap, the page labels the figure as a planning reference or declines to invent one.
  3. The counting method is part of the fact. A limit is meaningless without its unit. Each platform tool states whether it counts graphemes, code points, UTF-16 units, UTF-8 bytes, weighted characters or GSM-7 septets.
  4. Examples are tested. Worked examples in guides, such as weighted X counts or SMS segment totals, are checked against the same code the tools run.
  5. No invented statistics. Guides avoid unsourced claims about algorithms, engagement or “best” lengths. Where common advice is folklore rather than documentation, we say so.
  6. Dated reviews and corrections. Guides show when they were last reviewed. Platform rules change; when a reader reports an outdated limit, we check the current documentation and update the page, the tool and any guide that repeats the figure.

PostChars does not use AI to publish unreviewed articles. Content may be drafted with the help of writing tools, but every published fact is checked against its source and every example against the live tool before it goes on the site.

Privacy-first by design

The text you type or paste is processed in your browser. PostChars does not upload that text to an application server, and analytics events, where enabled with your consent, never include the content you enter. Optional draft saving uses browser-local storage only when you switch it on, and can be deleted with one button. You can change optional cookie and analytics choices at any time from “Privacy choices” in the footer. Details are in the Privacy Policy.

How PostChars is funded

The tools are free to use with no account or paywall. Running costs are kept low because the site is static and does its processing on your device. Any optional third-party services are described in the privacy policy and controlled through your privacy choices.
