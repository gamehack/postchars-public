Google Ads Character Limits: Headlines, Descriptions and Paths

A search ad can look great in a document and still fail a Google Ads field limit. Use this practical reference to check each responsive search ad (RSA) asset before you copy it into your account.

Open the free Google Ads Character Counter — check up to 15 headlines, 4 descriptions, two display paths and a sample live preview. No signup or upload.

Quick reference: RSA field limits

FieldMaximum lengthAssets
Headline30 characters3–15 headlines
Description90 characters2–4 descriptions
Display path15 characters per path fieldTwo optional fields

These are the responsive search ad limits, not responsive display ads or Performance Max asset limits. Google Ads may change its products; consult the official Google Ads responsive search ad help page when you publish.

Step 1: Write headlines that stand alone

Google can combine and reorder your headline assets. Avoid writing one headline that only makes sense after another. Start with at least three relevant headlines, preferably with a mix of user benefit, offer details and a clear call to action. The limit is 30 characters for each headline, not for all headlines combined.

For example, a writing-tool campaign might test the following independent angles: “Count Words Online”, “Free Character Counter”, and “Check Text Length Fast”. Treat these as drafting examples rather than promises about performance or approval. Avoid repeated identical headlines; diverse, accurate wording lets Google test more distinct combinations.

Headline examples: separate the 30-character limit from the message

For a fictional free text utility, three independent headline drafts could be Count Words Online, Check Text Length Free and Write With Clear Limits. Each expresses a different benefit and should be checked individually in the Google Ads responsive search ad character counter. These are example drafts, not approved ads or performance claims.

A common mistake is to count the three headlines together as a single 90-character allowance. The 30-character reference applies to each headline separately. Google can combine assets in different orders, so avoid sentence fragments that depend on another headline.

Step 2: Draft descriptions with a different job

A description has a 90-character limit. Use one to describe the product, and another to show a concrete benefit or next action. If you repeat the same claim in every description, the resulting combinations become less informative. Google may show more than one description but does not promise to display every asset together.

Description examples and checks

For the same fictional utility, a description draft could be Count words and characters in your browser. No sign-up needed. A complementary description could explain a different benefit, such as Review text length and copy your result without uploading your draft. Use the description counter to verify each against its own 90-character reference before publishing.

Step 3: Count double-width characters correctly

Google says that characters in double-width languages, including Chinese, Japanese and Korean, count as two toward these limits. That means the visible length of a mixed-language phrase may understate its effective advertising length. The PostChars counter provides an estimate of weighted characters; Google Ads is the final validator, especially for unusual Unicode and emoji sequences.

Step 4: Check optional display paths

Display paths help communicate where a visitor might land. Each of the two optional path fields has its own 15-character limit. Do not use a path preview as a substitute for checking the final URL and landing page. The URL needs to match the destination and follow Google Ads policies.

Step 5: Preview combinations and validate in Google Ads

A text preview is only illustrative: different devices, queries and auction contexts can change how the actual ad is assembled. Confirm that each headline reads well on its own, the descriptions make sense in more than one pairing, and the destination is accurate. Then copy your assets into the official editor to validate them before publishing.

Responsive search ads versus SEO snippets

The 30- and 90-character limits above are Google Ads asset field rules. They do not apply to the organic Google search title and meta description. For non-ad pages, use the SEO title and meta description preview instead. That tool is an illustrative snippet editor, not an ad validator.

Common questions

Does Google count spaces?

Yes, spaces and punctuation count toward the text-field limits. Double-width characters can count as two.

Can I use one headline and one description?

Responsive search ads are designed for multiple assets: Google specifies at least three headlines and two descriptions. See the latest official documentation for account-specific requirements.

Will the character counter create or publish an ad?

No. PostChars is an independent browser-based copy checking utility. It does not access your Google Ads account or transmit draft copy to PostChars.
