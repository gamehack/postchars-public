YouTube Titles and Descriptions: Limits, Chapters and Best Practices

A YouTube title can be up to 100 characters and a description up to 5,000, but viewers see only a fraction of either before deciding whether to watch. This guide covers the documented limits, the characters YouTube rejects, how chapters and hashtags work in the description, and a practical structure for writing both.

Open the YouTube Description Counter to check a description against the 5,000-character reference, or test a title in the cross-platform checker, which includes the 100-character YouTube title field.

The documented limits

FieldLimitNotes
Title100 charactersCannot contain < or >
Description5,000The YouTube Data API states 5,000 bytes; cannot contain < or >
Tags500 characters in totalCommas between tags count; a tag with a space counts as if wrapped in quotation marks
HashtagsMore than 60 and YouTube ignores all of themUp to three may be shown near the title

The title, description and tag rules come from the YouTube Data API reference for videos; the hashtag rules come from YouTube Help on hashtags. The byte wording matters if you upload through the API or a third-party tool: in UTF-8, plain English letters use one byte each, accented letters typically two, and Chinese, Japanese or Korean characters three, so a non-Latin description can reach a 5,000-byte limit well before 5,000 visible characters. YouTube Studio’s own counter is the final authority for text entered there.

Writing the title

The 100-character ceiling is rarely the problem. Titles are truncated in search results, recommendations and on phones long before 100 characters, and the exact cutoff depends on the surface and screen. Put the words that matter in the first half.

  1. Describe what the viewer gets. “How to Replace a Bike Chain in 10 Minutes” tells someone exactly what they will learn. “My Weekend Project!!” does not.
  2. Front-load the searchable phrase. If people search for “replace bike chain”, those words belong near the start, phrased naturally.
  3. Be accurate. YouTube’s misleading metadata policy prohibits titles that misrepresent the content. Viewers who feel misled leave quickly, which helps no one.
  4. Use capitals and symbols sparingly. One emphasized word can help; a title in full capitals is harder to read.
  5. Check it on a phone. Read only the first 40–50 characters. If that fragment makes no sense, reorder the title.

Before: “So I finally tried fixing my bike myself and honestly it went better than I expected (chain replacement)” — the useful words are at the end, where they will be cut off.
After: “Replace a Bike Chain at Home: Tools, Steps and Mistakes to Avoid” — 64 characters, with the topic first.

Writing the description

Only the first few lines of a description appear before viewers click to expand it, and the same opening can appear in search results. Treat those lines like the first paragraph of an article: say what the video covers and who it is for. The rest of the space is for viewers who want detail.

A practical description template

  1. One- or two-sentence summary of what the video shows, using the same words a viewer would search for.
  2. Key links mentioned in the video: the article, the product page, the source paper. Label each one so people know where it goes.
  3. Chapters (timestamps and titles), if the video has distinct sections.
  4. Context and corrections: tools used, errata, updates since filming.
  5. Disclosures for sponsorships or affiliate links, written plainly.
  6. Two or three relevant hashtags at the end, if you use them.

Note that for links in a description to be clickable in the YouTube interface, the channel must meet YouTube’s platform requirements, such as verification or access to advanced features.

Chapters: the rules that make them work

Chapters split a video into labelled sections with their own previews on the progress bar. According to YouTube Help on video chapters, a timestamp list in the description becomes chapters when:

  • the first timestamp is 00:00;
  • there are at least three timestamps, listed in ascending order;
  • each chapter is at least 10 seconds long.

Channels also need access to the feature, and creator-added chapters override automatic ones. A short example:

00:00 What you need
01:15 Removing the old chain
04:30 Sizing the new chain
07:05 Joining and testing

Chapter titles are part of the description’s character count. Keep them short and descriptive; they also act as a table of contents for viewers scanning the page.

Hashtags and tags

Hashtags in a title or description become links to a results page for that hashtag. YouTube may show up to three of the hashtags from your description near the title. YouTube’s policy says not to over-tag: if a video has more than 60 hashtags, all of them are ignored, and adding unrelated or misleading hashtags can lead to removal. Hashtags cannot contain spaces.

Tags, entered in a separate field, are limited to 500 characters in total, counting commas. YouTube Help on tags says the title, thumbnail and description matter more, and that tags play a minimal role in discovery except when your topic is commonly misspelled. Spend your effort on the title and description first.

Common mistakes

  • Keyword lists in the description. YouTube’s hashtag policy specifically warns against adding ordinary descriptive tags or repetitive sentences to the description.
  • A description that starts with links. A viewer seeing “https://…” as the first line learns nothing about the video.
  • Angle brackets. < and > are not allowed in titles or descriptions; replace “<3” or arrow notation with words.
  • Copy-pasted boilerplate that pushes the video-specific summary below the fold.

Frequently asked questions

How long should a YouTube title be?

Long enough to describe the video clearly, with the key words early. Many effective titles fall well under 70 characters, but there is no official ideal length; the hard limit is 100.

Do emoji count toward the description limit?

Yes. Each emoji is part of the text, and many use four bytes in UTF-8, which matters where a limit is measured in bytes.

Why are my chapters not showing?

Check that the first timestamp is 00:00, that there are at least three in ascending order, that each section is at least 10 seconds, and that your channel has access to the feature.
