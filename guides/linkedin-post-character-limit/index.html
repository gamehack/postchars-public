LinkedIn Post Character Limit: 3,000 Characters Explained

LinkedIn's Help Center specifies a 3,000-character limit for a post. That is a character limit, not a word limit. It applies to the text of a standard post or update; long-form LinkedIn articles are a different publishing format.

What is LinkedIn's post character limit?

The official LinkedIn Help: Post and share updates document lists a maximum of 3,000 characters per post. This is the correct reference for ordinary profile updates. A post near that boundary is much longer than a typical short announcement, so length alone does not tell you whether people will read it.

Characters, words and line breaks are not the same

A character counter measures text length; a word counter measures word-like units. Spaces, punctuation, hashtags and line breaks all contribute to the content that you type. Some emoji consist of more than one Unicode code point, even though they look like one symbol. That is why a visible-character count can differ from a platform's own internal validation.

A practical four-step editing workflow

  1. Lead with the point. Write one opening sentence that names the problem or insight without requiring a reader to open the full post.
  2. Keep paragraphs skimmable. Separate an example, the explanation and the takeaway with sensible line breaks. Avoid inserting empty lines merely to look longer.
  3. Remove repetition. Re-read the draft and delete sentences that restate your opening without adding evidence or a useful example.
  4. Check length and preview. Paste the result into the LinkedIn post counter; then confirm formatting in LinkedIn before publishing.

When should you publish an article instead?

Use a regular post for a focused takeaway, question, announcement or short demonstration. If your explanation cannot fit without sacrificing important context, consider a LinkedIn article or a linked page rather than forcing the entire explanation into a 3,000-character post. LinkedIn's help documentation explicitly distinguishes over-limit posts from articles.

Frequently asked questions

Does LinkedIn allow more than 3,000 characters?

Not for the ordinary post format covered by the cited Help Center page. Other formats, including articles, have their own rules. Limits can change, so check the composer when publishing.

Do line breaks count toward a LinkedIn post?

Line breaks are part of the underlying text. If your draft is close to the maximum, leave some room for changes after pasting.

Is the best LinkedIn post exactly 3,000 characters long?

No. A limit is a ceiling, not a recommended target. Prefer the shortest length that includes the evidence, context and action a reader needs.

Does PostChars publish the post for me?

No. The counter is a local drafting aid, not a LinkedIn publishing integration. Copy your finished text into LinkedIn to publish.
